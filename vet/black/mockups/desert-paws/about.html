Dr. Elena Márquez, DVM

Co-owner & chief of staff · Exotic companion medicine

Elena grew up in North Las Vegas translating for her parents at doctors' offices, and she has never forgotten what it feels like to be the family that does not quite understand what is happening. She opened Desert Paws in 2014 with Dr. Okafor on two conditions: that the practice stay independent, and that nobody would ever be handed a bill they had not already agreed to.

She trained in exotic companion medicine because Las Vegas is full of bearded dragons, conures and rabbits whose owners were being turned away by every dog-and-cat clinic in the valley. Today roughly one visit in six here is an exotic.

Three things a corporate group would not let us do.

Hold slots open

A chain fills every appointment slot weeks out because utilisation is the metric. We deliberately leave same-day openings unbooked, every day including weekends, so the dog who ate a skewer this morning can be seen this afternoon.

Publish the price

Our exam fees are on the website: $59 standard, $95 urgent, $39 for a new client's first visit. Anything past the exam is quoted in writing before we start, and we phone you before any number changes.

Say yes to the reptile

Exotics take longer, need specific training, and do not fit a fifteen-minute template. That is exactly why most clinics decline them — and exactly why we do not.

What a visit actually looks like.

1

You call or book

A person answers — in English or Spanish. If it is urgent we find you a slot that day rather than putting you on a waitlist.

2

We examine and explain

The doctor examines your pet, tells you what they think is going on in plain language, and writes down what it will cost before anything else happens.

3

You decide, then we treat

Nothing proceeds until you have agreed to the written plan. If circumstances change mid-visit, we stop and call you.
