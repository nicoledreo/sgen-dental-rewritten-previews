What we do and don't take.

Reptiles

Bearded dragons, leopard geckos, corn and king snakes, tortoises. Mouth rot, MBD, retained sheds, prolapse, egg binding. Every reptile visit includes a husbandry review — most problems we see are enclosure problems.

Birds

Conures, cockatiels, budgies, African greys, lovebirds. Feather-destructive behaviour, respiratory disease, beak and nail work, egg binding. We work them up properly rather than writing it off as "that's parrots".

Pocket pets

Rabbits, guinea pigs, rats, hamsters, ferrets. GI stasis, dental spurs, mites, respiratory infection. Rabbits and guinea pigs get a dedicated quiet room away from the dogs.

What we don't take

No venomous species, no primates, no large livestock. If your animal is outside what we can do well, we will say so on the phone and point you at someone who can — rather than book you and find out later.

Questions we get about services.

Are you a 24-hour emergency hospital?
No, and we will never imply otherwise. We are a day practice with same-day urgent capacity. Our line is answered during open hours; outside them it gives you the two nearest 24-hour emergency hospitals with directions, and we follow up the next morning to pick your pet's care back up.
Do I need an appointment for urgent care?
Preferably call first so we can prepare, but no — walk in. We hold same-day slots specifically for urgent cases. Routine wellness visits are by appointment so nobody waits three hours behind an emergency.
Is the rattlesnake vaccine worth it?
If your dog hikes, runs off-leash near desert scrub, or lives near open land, we recommend it. It buys time and reduces severity — it does not replace emergency treatment. If your dog is bitten, call us on the way in; do not wait to see how it develops.
