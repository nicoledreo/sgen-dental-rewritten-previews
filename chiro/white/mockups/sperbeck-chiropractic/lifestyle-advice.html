Lifestyle Advice in Tracy, CA

Guidance on how you sit, sleep and work, so the adjustment is not fighting your day.

One of the six services this practice provides.

Guidance, not a prescription

We offer chiropractic care and lifestyle guidance.

An adjustment takes a few minutes. Your desk gets eight hours. If the second one is what caused the problem, the first one is going to keep losing.

That is the whole reason this page exists.

The desk and the driver's seat

At the desk

  • Monitor at eye height, so your neck is not tipped forward all day
  • Feet flat on the floor, not tucked under the chair
  • Stand up every half hour or so, even briefly

In the driver's seat

This matters here because so many of our patients drive for a living or are recovering from a collision.

  • Sit back into the seat rather than perching forward
  • Bring the seat close enough that you are not reaching for the wheel
  • On a long drive, stop and get out. Twenty minutes of standing beats another hour of sitting.

Sleep, and getting out of bed

Sleep on your side or your back. Not on your front. Your pillow should keep your neck level with your spine. Too high is as bad as too flat.

  • RollRoll onto your side.
  • DropDrop your legs over the edge.
  • PushPush yourself up with your arm.

Most people twist and sit straight up. That is the movement that catches a sore back.

Moving between visits

Walking is the default recommendation.

While you are sore, short and frequent beats one long walk. Three ten-minute walks are easier on a painful back than one thirty-minute one.

If a movement sharpens the pain, report it at the next visit. The same principle runs through corrective exercises.

What we will not tell you how to live

We do not lecture you about your weight, your smoking, your job or your family.

If a habit is genuinely driving the pain, Dr. Sperbeck will say so once, plainly, and then leave it alone.

One doctor's advice, not a handout

This is advice given face to face by Dr. Charles Sperbeck, D.C., after thirty years treating people in this town.

He knows what your job is, and what he told you last visit. So the advice gets adjusted rather than repeated.

That is the difference between advice and a printed sheet. More on Dr. Sperbeck.

How this shows up in a visit

It is not a separate appointment and not a separate charge.

It is a couple of minutes at the end of an adjustment.

It is fitted to the person. What a pregnant patient is told is not what a roofer is told, and Dr. Sperbeck is gentle enough to work with older patients, children and pregnant patients.

And when the issue is medical rather than musculoskeletal, he will work with your medical doctor, or refer you on. See also nutritional counseling.
