What "pinched nerve" actually means

It is an everyday phrase, not a diagnosis. It describes a nerve that has become irritated or inflamed somewhere along its path: through tight muscle, through a narrowed joint space, or beside a swollen disc.

What people feel: burning, pins and needles, numbness, weakness, and pain that runs rather than sits.

The description on its own does not tell anyone the cause. That is why a visit starts with an examination rather than with a treatment plan.

03

The hands-on work that usually comes first

  • Soft tissue first, in order

    Trigger point work, stretching, then deep tissue and massage therapy to release the tissue around the nerve.

  • Adjusting comes after that

    Gentle low-force adjusting comes after that, and only where the examination supports it. You set the pressure. If it is too much, say so and he eases off.

Dr. Sperbeck is gentle enough to work with older patients, children, and pregnant patients.
